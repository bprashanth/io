How the rates are calculated (formulas)

1. Training completion rate (%)

completed_training ÷ women_enrolled × 100

Of the women enrolled in a district in that year, how many completed the training.

2. Employment after 6 months — out of all enrolled (%)

employed_at_6_months ÷ women_enrolled × 100

Of all women enrolled in a district in that year, how many were employed at 6 months.

3. Employment after 6 months — out of training completers (%)

employed_at_6_months ÷ completed_training × 100

Of the women who completed the training, how many were employed at 6 months.

The data file does not say which group employment is measured against, so both possible employment rates (2 and 3) are shown with clearly different labels. They give different numbers, and neither is marked as “official”. Rates are rounded to 1 decimal place; changes are calculated from the unrounded values.

Reading these figures

  • The counts are raw numbers from the file; the percentages are calculated from them using the formulas above.
  • These figures show patterns, not causes. The file does not say why a district's rate is higher or lower, what the training consisted of, or what jobs were available, so no “why” can be answered from this data alone.
  • “Employed at 6 months” is a snapshot count. The file does not document how each woman was followed up, so treat it as the programme's reported number.

Why is Purnia lower than Nalanda? (2023) — what this file can and cannot tell us

What the 2023 rows can tell us (arithmetic on the counts)

  • Employment at 6 months happens in two stages: a woman must first complete the training, then be employed after completing it. The employment rate out of all enrolled is the product of the other two rates. Check with the 2023 counts: Purnia 73.0% × 63.0% = 46.0% (598 ÷ 1,300); Nalanda 87.0% × 74.7% = 65.0% (650 ÷ 1,000).
  • Purnia is behind Nalanda at both stages: completion rate 73.0% vs 87.0% (14.0 pp lower) and employment among completers 63.0% vs 74.7% (11.7 pp lower). The overall 19.0 pp employment gap is a combination of both stages; the file cannot say how much of the 19.0 pp each stage caused.
  • The same pattern is in 2022 (completion 70.0% vs 85.0%; employment out of all enrolled 42.0% vs 60.0%), so it is not a one-year effect.

What this file cannot tell us

  • Why Purnia's completion rate is lower — there are no drop-out reasons, attendance, or training delivery details in the file.
  • Why fewer of Purnia's completers were employed — there are no job-offer, job-type, wage, or labour-market columns in the file.
  • Whether the two districts' "employed at 6 months" figures were measured the same way — the file does not document the follow-up method.

What would be needed to answer "why" and to choose an intervention

  • Training records by district: drop-out reasons, attendance, what the training consisted of, schedule, fees, trainers.
  • Placement records: how many job offers there were, what kind, which employers, wages, and whether the programme or the women themselves found the jobs.
  • The follow-up method: how "employed at 6 months" was determined and the response rate.
  • Separate district labour-market evidence (not in this file).

This file alone cannot say which intervention to make in Purnia. All the file supports is that Purnia's gap appears at both stages (completing training, and being employed after completing), so a response aimed at only one stage would not close the full gap.
