# Field note: what the blind test taught us before anyone voted at the event

We built the comparison instrument we wished existed: every question in io fans out to
three models over the same coded data, the answers come back as three unnamed cards, and
the person must choose - with a reason - before they can ask anything else. Votes land on
a projector board. Nobody knows which model they are judging.

Running it ourselves against a graded answer key produced the finding that will anchor
the event's story. For language work - explaining a dataset, rewriting for a donor - the
laptop-class 9B tied or beat the big models, and paying a premium there is waste. For
interpreting data, the mid and frontier tiers start earning their cost. For dashboards,
frontier won every blind round, once against a 9B that spent fourteen minutes producing a
page of zeros. And for precise arithmetic over a wide table, every tier failed the same
sum - the answer to that class of question is not a bigger model but local computation
with receipts, which is the event's deeper argument.

The instrument also keeps catching things beside its purpose: a fabricated total exposed
because one card computed 1,447 while the file's own prose claimed 1,842; a real leak
(a surname escaping past a sentence-ending period) found because testing like a human
means reading what actually left. The night with the 81-gigabyte streamed brain closed
the loop: enormous models can plan honestly at walking pace, but the event's portable
intelligence is a dense 27B carried whole on a drive - and the room's votes, not our
opinions, will say whether the premium tiers are worth it.
